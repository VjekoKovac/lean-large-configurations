The underlying coordinates are `Fin d → ℝ`. Their volume is product Lebesgue measure, and `NullMeasurableSet` expresses measurability in its completion. The written coordinate distance agrees with mathlib's finite ℓᵖ distance by `lpDistance_eq_piLp_dist`. The ratio uses the intersection with the closed centered cube of real side length $R$. Its numerator has finite measure and the ratio lies in $[0,1]$ for $R>0$. Thus the real-valued filter liminf is well-defined in the usual sense. The actual density takes the limit over all real sides tending to infinity. For Theorems 1–2, the finite Fourier and Fejér estimates give enough indices in every test arc, uniformly over the lower coefficients. The counting argument selects exactly $n$ distinct indices. Translating the origin of a half-open test arc puts its hit strictly inside an arc of twice the length. This changes the degree-dependent constant only. Strict convexity then parametrizes every putative axial copy. The even case uses a single shell. The odd case intersects the shells for all signs before inspecting a copy, giving the factor $2^d$ in the lower-density estimate. Theorem 1 follows by specializing the even clause. The uploaded Proposition 5 begins at degree two, while the arXiv version also states degree one. The three targets only require degree two and above.
